What is happening?

The fold-and-cut theorem says any straight-line shape can be made by folding one sheet of paper flat and making one complete straight cut.

This version no longer invents a fold order from an arbitrary face tree. It uses the theorem solver’s ordered corridor reflections: each crease carries the entire remaining packet of faces, and independent corridor operations are grouped into compound folds.

The paper is rendered as rigid faces rotating about crease hinges in 3D. If the geometry solver cannot produce a usable construction, the app reports that instead of substituting an illustrative fake.

Geometry foundation: Danielle Wang’s MIT-licensed 2017 Fold & Cut implementation for MIT 6.849, based on the straight-skeleton method of Demaine, Demaine & Lubiw.
